The rules behind the design

Hover PR chips, avatars and online faces for their tooltips. Everything clickable shows a pointer.

Pull request state: one chip, picked by priority

A task shows the first state below that is true for its PR, as an icon, a word and the PR number (a link to GitHub). The tooltip lists everything else that is true. A task with two PRs shows two chips.

Avatars are the defining element

Three kinds of face. People are circles: a photo, or initials on their identity colour. Projects are rounded squares: an uploaded image, or initials on their colour. Agents are rounder squares that always carry their role's glyph, never letters. When an agent works for a person it sits on that person's avatar, pulsing while live; a green ring means online.

Four kinds of button, and links only navigate

v1 mixed a filled "Answer" with a grey "Decide" in the same list, text-only "Take over" beside a filled "Pause", and "Update branch" as a link. Now:

one per view or panel
every other action
row and toolbar actions
red text; solid only in its confirmation

Cost is a total; hover for what it is made of

Every cost on screen is model tokens plus machine time. The number is the total; a hairline under it shows the split at a glance, and its tooltip gives both parts with their units. Machine cost is each run's time on a lux host multiplied by that host's rate (lux already records a run's time and CPU seconds).

Colour: four families, each with one job — fixed from v1

  • Accent blue is interaction only: primary buttons, links, focus, selected tab. v1 also used it for the "running" dot — running now uses the info tone's mark, which the design system already defines.
  • Tones are status only. v1 invented a darker red and used a grey "wait" style for an amber warning; both now come from the real tones.
  • Roles are agents only, from the real role palette. v1 invented a purple "fixer" — a fixer is an implementer, so it now wears the implementer's colour with a wrench glyph.
  • Identity colours are for faces — people and projects — from the real eight slots, picked by hashing the id (a project can pick its own). Never used for status. v1 approximated them.
  • One new token: merged violet, because every GitHub user reads violet as merged.

Project: epics in progress, planned and done

  • In progress: the full cards, with everyone working in them and what their agents are doing right now.
  • Planned (not started) and Done sit side by side below as quiet lists — who leads them, how big they are, and for Done what they cost and how long they took.

Task: overview, findings, sessions, activity

  • Two PRs (the task touched two repositories), each with its state, checks, reviews and the actions GitHub would give you: merge, update branch, re-run checks, request review.
  • Findings lists every review finding with where it is, who found it, and what became of it. Sessions is every agent's transcript, with its tools, plan and files.
  • Plan: an agent's todo list is pinned above its session's chat — one line (progress, the step it's on) that opens to the whole list. The running step on Overview, cards and the sidebar show its progress too.
  • Files: everything the task's sessions saved — images and video as a gallery, documents as a list — each opening in a viewer with its versions, and one-click download (or all as a zip).

Settings, in three places

  • Your settings (click your name, bottom left): profile, keys, notifications — everyone.
  • Organisation settings (above your name): members, GitHub, and the defaults for agents and delivery — organisation admins only. No projects in here.
  • Project settings (a project's ⋯ menu in the sidebar, or its Settings button): the same pages, with every value marked “From Acme” or “Overridden”, one-click reset — project admins.
  • Agents: pick a role under Agents in the left menu. Its prompt reads as a document; Edit turns it into Markdown source in place, Save or Cancel turns it back.

Three rules, and what they replace

Today the design system asks for 6px radii, 8px cards and 12px dialogs, and 16 components draw a full hairline box. Stack a card in a column in a panel and you see three nested rounded outlines. The proposal keeps the palette, type, spacing and motion as they are, and changes only the shape language.

1 · Structure is square. Only what you touch or what floats is round.

Panels, board columns, cards, tables, the pipeline, the chat: 0 radius. Buttons, fields, segmented controls, menu rows: 6px. Dialogs, popovers, toasts: 10px. Avatars and the one "needs you" badge: full.

2 · Lines are for fields and focus, not for separating things.

Regions are told apart by shade and space (the README already says so; the CSS drifted). A hairline survives in exactly three places: an input's edge, the focus ring, and a diff's gutter. Table rows get air, not rules; hover gives them a wash.

3 · Status is a glyph and a word, not a pill.

Every "Completed" / "Running" chip today is a rounded box with a border and a tint. In a pipeline of eight steps that is eight boxes. Status becomes a coloured mark and a coloured word. The only filled pill left is Needs you — so it is, again, the loudest thing on the screen.

4 · Every person has a face and a name.

Humans get an identity-coloured round avatar and their name everywhere an action is shown — chat, events, timeline, toasts, conflict notices. Agents keep a rounded-square role tile, so a person and an agent never look alike. "Human" and "a person" disappear from the UI.
